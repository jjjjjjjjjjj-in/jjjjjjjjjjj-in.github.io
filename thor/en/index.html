Good to know before watchingNo spoilers

They're based on myths

Thor, Loki and Odin come from Norse mythology. Here they're shown as extremely powerful beings from another world.

It moves between space and Earth

The settings are Asgard, the frost giants' realm of Jotunheim, and New Mexico.

Watch Loki

Thor's brother Loki becomes one of the MCU's most popular villains. Watching how his feelings shift makes the film more fun.

Stay until after the credits

An important scene after the credits leads into The Avengers.

StorySpoilers

Read the story (spoilers up to the ending)

1. A man from the storm

In the New Mexico desert, scientist Jane and her team are studying a strange storm when a man falls from the sky. The story then jumps back to Asgard.

2. Prince Thor's mistake

On the day Thor is to be crowned, frost giants sneak into Asgard's vault. Furious, Thor ignores his father Odin and leads his friends to attack the frost giants' realm, starting a fight.

3. Banished to Earth

Odin strips Thor of his power and banishes him to Earth. He sends the hammer too, enchanted so that only someone worthy can lift it.

4. Thor on Earth

Powerless, Thor is taken in by Jane and her friends. He breaks into the S.H.I.E.L.D. site to get his hammer back, but can't lift it.

5. Loki's secret

In Asgard, Loki learns he's really a frost giant's son, raised by Odin. When Odin falls into a deep sleep, Loki takes the throne and lies to Thor that their father is dead and he can't come home.

6. Worthy

Loki sends the Destroyer to Earth. Thor offers himself to protect the townspeople. That selflessness proves him worthy, the hammer returns, and his power comes back.

7. Brother against brother

Back in Asgard, Thor destroys the rainbow bridge to stop Loki from wiping out the frost giants' realm. Loki falls into the void of space.

8. Worlds apart

With the bridge gone, Thor can't get back to Jane on Earth. After the credits, Fury shows Dr. Selvig a glowing blue cube.

Key terms

Asgard
The realm of the gods where Thor lives, linked to other worlds by a rainbow bridge.
Mjolnir
Thor's hammer. Odin's enchantment means only someone "worthy" can lift it.
The Bifrost
The bridge between Asgard and other worlds, guarded by Heimdall.
Jotunheim and the Frost Giants
The realm of the frost giants, old enemies of Asgard, ruled by King Laufey.
The Odinsleep
A deep sleep Odin falls into to restore his strength. Someone else rules while he sleeps.
The Destroyer
A giant suit of metal armor that guards Asgard's vault. Loki sends it to Earth.
S.H.I.E.L.D.
The secret U.S. agency that comes to investigate where the hammer landed, led here by Coulson.

Confusing parts, explainedSpoilers

Contains spoilers about the ending.

Is Thor a god?

In myth, yes. In this film, Asgardians are long-lived, very powerful beings from another world, whom ancient people on Earth took for gods.

Why does Loki turn bad?

He learns he's a frost giant's son and that the truth was hidden from him. His longing for his father's approval gets tangled up with anger.

Why could Thor lift the hammer again?

By offering his own life to protect others, he proved himself worthy under Odin's enchantment.

Who's the archer watching the hammer?

S.H.I.E.L.D. agent Clint Barton, Hawkeye. He only appears briefly here, and gets a bigger role in The Avengers.

What's the blue cube at the end?

The Tesseract from Captain America: The First Avenger. Dr. Selvig is asked to study it, which leads into The Avengers.

Odin's words

Dr. Selvig returns

Jane's mentor Dr. Selvig plays a big part in The Avengers.
