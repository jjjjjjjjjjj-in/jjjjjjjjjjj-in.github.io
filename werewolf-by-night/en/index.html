Black and white

A little scary

Expect monsters and a spooky mood.

My takeOpinion

An hour-long special made in the style of an old black-and-white monster movie. It's short and stands alone, so I'd recommend it as an easy oddity.

StorySpoilers

Read the story (spoilers up to the ending)

Part 1: Bloodstone Manor

After legendary hunter Ulysses Bloodstone dies, his widow Verussa summons five hunters, including Jack Russell, to the manor.

His estranged daughter Elsa returns too, and joins the hunt despite Verussa's warning.

Whoever kills the monster loose in the maze and claims the Bloodstone embedded in it becomes the new leader.

Part 2: The hunt in the maze

In the maze, Elsa kills the hunter Liorn.

The monster turns out to be Jack's friend, the swamp creature Ted; Jack posed as a hunter to rescue him.

Jack and Elsa team up, free Ted and take the stone for Elsa. But the stone reacts violently to Jack: he is a monster too.

Part 3: The werewolf

Verussa cages them and uses the stone to turn Jack into a werewolf, hoping he'll kill Elsa.

Instead the werewolf breaks out and kills the guards, while Elsa kills the last two hunters. The werewolf spares Elsa and leaves, and Ted burns Verussa as she tries to shoot Elsa.

Part 4: After the night

Elsa inherits the Bloodstone and the manor, and the picture turns from black and white to color.

The next morning, Jack wakes in human form in the forest with Ted watching over him, glad to hear Elsa is safe.

Confusing parts, explainedSpoilers

Contains spoilers about the ending.

Who is Jack really?

Not a hunter, but a werewolf. He joined the hunt to free his friend, the captured creature Ted.

Why did Elsa leave home?

She never fit her family's monster-hunting ways and was estranged from her father.

Easy-to-miss details

Old-movie style

Scratches on the film and theatrical touches evoke 1930s horror movies.

A flash of red

In the black-and-white world, the Bloodstone glows red.
